AI Instruction Following and Conflict Resolution Guide

1. Identify the User’s Instructions

Break the prompt into individual requirements.

Check for:

* Required topic and task.
* Intended audience.
* Word or sentence limits.
* Formatting requirements.
* Required information.
* Prohibited content or claims.
* Tone and level of detail.

2. Identify Conflicting Instructions

Ask:

* Can all requirements be satisfied simultaneously?
* Do formatting requirements contradict one another?
* Are there incompatible length requirements?
* Does one instruction prevent another from being completed?

When instructions genuinely conflict, the AI should explain the problem and ask the user which requirement to prioritize when clarification is necessary.

3. Identify Ambiguous Instructions

Look for unclear requirements such as:

* “Make it short.”
* “Explain in detail.”
* “Use simple language.”
* “Make it professional.”

Determine whether reasonable interpretation is possible or clarification is needed.

4. Evaluate Each Instruction Independently

Check whether the response:

* Follows the requested format.
* Meets the word or sentence limit.
* Addresses the intended audience.
* Includes all required information.
* Avoids prohibited statements.
* Completes the requested task.

5. Evaluate Factual Accuracy Separately

Ask:

* Are the claims factually correct?
* Are there unsupported statements?
* Are absolute claims justified?
* Does the explanation contain misleading information?

Do not automatically classify a response as factually incorrect simply because it fails an instruction.

6. Compare and Rank Responses

Use these five evaluation criteria:

Criterion	What to Evaluate
Accuracy	Is the information factually correct?
Relevance	Does it address the user’s request?
Completeness	Does it include all important requested information?
Clarity	Is it understandable and well organized?
Instruction Following	Does it satisfy the user’s requirements?

Rank responses according to their overall performance and explain the most important differences.

7. Write Constructive Evaluator Feedback

Good feedback should:

* Identify what the response did correctly.
* Specify which instructions were violated.
* Explain factual errors when present.
* Recommend concrete improvements.
* Avoid vague judgments.

Final Quality Checklist

* [ ]	I identified every user instruction.
* [ ]	I checked for conflicting requirements.
* [ ]	I identified ambiguous wording.
* [ ]	I evaluated each requirement independently.
* [ ]	I assessed factual accuracy separately.
* [ ]	I checked relevance and completeness.
* [ ]	I compared responses using consistent criteria.
* [ ]	I explained my ranking decision clearly.

Conclusion

Reliable AI evaluation requires careful interpretation of user instructions, independent assessment of evaluation criteria, and clear explanations of response strengths and weaknesses.
